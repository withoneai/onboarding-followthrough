# Our onboarding plan

This file is the whole judgment configuration. The model reads it verbatim for
every hire. The code that reads Asana and the calendar does not read it, so
edit it freely: it changes what counts as a gap, not how the facts are found.

## By day 14

Every new hire, whatever the role, should have all of these by the end of
their second week. Each line is checked against the Asana project and the
hire's calendar.

- Laptop in hand and every system they need granted by day 2. This is the one that blocks everything else.
- A 1:1 with their manager every week, the first one inside week one.
- An onboarding buddy assigned and met at least once in week one.
- Security and privacy training completed by day 5.
- Payroll and benefits enrollment done by day 10. The benefits window closes on day 30 and cannot be reopened.
- An intro meeting with each person on their team in the first two weeks.
- Their first real piece of work owned: a shipped change for engineers, three shadowed calls for sales, live tickets answered for support.
- A 30-60-90 plan written and agreed with their manager.

## What counts as a gap

- A plan item that is overdue and not done is a gap. Name who has to act.
- A meeting the plan expects that is missing from the calendar, cancelled, or declined by the hire is a gap. A meeting that was moved and then happened is not.
- Something done late is not a gap anymore. Mention it only if it was the laptop and access line, because a late start there usually explains the rest.
- Something not due yet is not a gap, unless it depends on a blocked item and will obviously miss its date.
- If the calendar could not be read, say so plainly and do not call any meeting missing. Judge the tasks alone.
- Do not chase the hire for anything another person was supposed to give them. Chase the person who owes it.

## Status

- on_track: nothing overdue that matters, the meetings are happening.
- slipping: one or two gaps, none of them blocking.
- at_risk: the laptop and access line is open, or the hire has gone a full week without a 1:1 with their manager, or three or more gaps.

## How to write the chase

- One or two sentences to the person who owes the work, written so they can act on it from the message alone.
- Name the hire, the item, and the date it was due.
- Ask for one concrete thing: a date, a booking, a yes. Not "any update?".
- Friendly and plain. No blame, no exclamation marks.
